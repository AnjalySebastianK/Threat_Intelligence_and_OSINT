# Understanding Threat Actors

## Cyber Criminals
- Cyber criminals are individuals or groups who engage in illegal activities using digital systems.
- Their primary motivation is financial gain, and they often exploit vulnerabilities in networks, applications, or human behavior to steal money, data, or valuable assets.
- Common activities include ransomware attacks, credit card fraud, identity theft, and selling stolen information on underground markets.
- Cyber criminals operate globally, often using anonymization tools to hide their identities, and they continuously adapt their methods to bypass security controls.

## Nation-State Actors
- Nation-state actors are cyber operators who act on behalf of a government.
- Their objectives are not limited to financial gain but extend to political, military, and strategic advantages.
- These actors are highly resourced, with access to advanced tools, training, and intelligence. They may target critical infrastructure, government agencies, or large corporations to steal sensitive data, disrupt services, or conduct espionage.
- Nation-state actors are considered some of the most dangerous adversaries because their campaigns are persistent, well-funded, and often protected by their own governments.

## Insider Threats
- Insider threats originate from individuals within an organization who misuse their access to harm the company. These insiders may be employees, contractors, or business partners.
- Their motivations vary from financial gain and revenge to negligence or coercion.
- Insider threats are particularly challenging because insiders already have legitimate access to systems and data, making detection more difficult.
- Examples include employees stealing intellectual property, leaking confidential information, or unintentionally exposing sensitive data through careless actions.

## Hacktivists
- Hacktivists are individuals or groups who use hacking techniques to promote political, social, or ideological causes. Unlike cyber criminals, their primary motivation is not financial but rather activism.
- Hacktivists often target government websites, corporations, or institutions they oppose, using methods such as defacement, denial-of-service attacks, or data leaks.
- Their goal is to raise awareness, embarrass their targets, or push for social change. While hacktivism may not always cause financial loss, it can damage reputations and disrupt operations.

## Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)
- Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) are highly sophisticated and long-term cyber campaigns carried out by organized groups, often linked to nation-states.
- The term “persistent” reflects their ability to remain undetected within a network for extended periods, sometimes months or years. APTs use advanced techniques such as zero-day exploits, custom malware, and stealthy lateral movement to achieve their objectives.
- Their goals typically include espionage, data theft, or sabotage.
-  APTs are considered one of the most serious threats because they combine patience, resources, and technical expertise to infiltrate and maintain access to high-value targets.

## Summary
- Threat actors vary in their motivations, resources, and methods.
- Cyber criminals seek financial gain, nation-state actors pursue strategic and political objectives, insiders exploit their trusted positions, hacktivists promote ideological causes, and APTs conduct long-term, sophisticated campaigns.
- Understanding these categories helps organizations anticipate different types of attacks and design defenses that address both external and internal risks.
- By recognizing the unique characteristics of each threat actor, security teams can prioritize responses and strengthen resilience against evolving adversaries.
